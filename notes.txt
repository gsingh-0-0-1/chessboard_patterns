I've been thinking about trying to predict the structure of a 2D region from the corresponding 
player attack patterns. If we take this problem into one dimension, where the players are playing 
on simply an infinite linear strip rather than an infinite 2D grid, we can then think about wrapping
this 1D strip around in a spiral to create a 2D board. Then, we can see that areas on this 2D board
that correspond to large regions of pieces belonging to the same player occur when, in the 
one-dimensional case, pieces belonging to that player occur with a frequency equivalent to that
of the winding frequency of the spiral. Moreover, the lower the difference is between the rate 
of change of the winding frequency and the rate of change of the frequency of those pieces occcuring(*1),
the larger the region ends up being. Naively, it seems like a DFT could help do some predictive 
thinking here, or at the very least, it could help understand which types of attack patterns
produce what kinds of structural patterns.

I'm working through a refactor right now that should let me manipulate 1D board arrays into spirals
a bit more easily -- really, it should let me consider the n-dimensional case of a problem like this.
Then I might be able to start thinking about predicting structure.


(*1) the rate of change of the winding frequency wrt. the radius
